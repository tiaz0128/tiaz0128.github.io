## People Hungry for Growth

![Developers who value growth](/assets/img/content/insight/006/002.webp){:.img-s}

*> Source: [platum.kr - "What Matters More to Developers Than Salary!"](https://platum.kr/archives/193558){:target="_blank"}*{:.img-caption}

The world is changing fast, and developers seem to need to sense and keep up with those changes more than anyone. Here are the common questions I've heard from the **people hungry for growth**{:.orange} I've met over the years.

- "I don't know where to start"
- "I don't have a senior mentor at work"
- "I don't know where to get information"

For anyone with questions like these, I want to share what I know about **'how developers find insight'**.

## Getting Information: Many Channels

The fastest way to pick up information and sense change is through social media (SNS, _Social Network Service_). But there are so many channels these days that information is fragmented, which is a downside.

Often a post that's on one channel isn't on another. So I recommend **following as many as you can**{:.orange} on each channel at first, and then **dropping them one by one**{:.orange} as you find the ones that don't suit you.

There are so many channels that I'll just note each one's character and the main ones. ~~I'll also keep a list of the channels I know on a separate page.~~ (I'll link it once I make it.🤣)

## How to Study: Courses and Books

### Online Courses

These seem like a great way to understand technology more easily and keep up with fast-moving trends. The downsides are that they take a lot of time, and it's hard to find where the content you want is.

- [Inflearn](https://www.inflearn.com/){:.none target="_blank"}: the most well-rounded courses and prices.
- [Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/){:.none target="_blank"}: courses on countless topics at reasonable prices; subtitles are supported but a bit lacking.
- [Fast Campus](https://fastcampus.co.kr/){:.none target="_blank"}: covers specific topics in depth. People tend to either love it or not.
- [Codeit](https://www.codeit.kr/){:.none target="_blank"}: a subscription lets you take whatever courses you want.
- [Nomad Coders](https://nomadcoders.co/){:.none target="_blank"}: there are sites run by individuals, too.
- There are also YouTube members-only videos.

### Offline Courses

There are mostly weekday-evening/weekend offline courses run by academies. Look for the course you want through [Work24 - Job Training Search](http://hrd.go.kr/hrdp/ma/pmmao/indexNew.do){:.none target="_blank"} and sign up!

### Books

Books let you use the table of contents to quickly learn just the part you need. The downside is that they can't reflect trends quickly. Still, I recommend you read the **books that count as classics**{:.orange} in the development field.

Also, social media channels often run 'book review events' and 'beta reader recruitment', so there are plenty of chances to read books for free! Be sure to follow the publishers!

- [Awesome Devbook](https://github.com/738/awesome-devbook){:.none target="_blank"}: a list of Korean development books.
- [Seoul Library e-Library](https://elib.seoul.go.kr/main){:.none target="_blank"}: borrow e-books for free, and there are more quality titles than you'd think.
- Follow publishers on social media and join book review events and beta reading

## Presenting

Giving talks and lectures goes beyond just sharing knowledge; it also does a lot for your career and your network. There's no better place to practice presenting than a study group. I recommend practicing as much as you can, with no pressure.

### Finding Chances to Present

- **Practice in study groups as if it were the real thing**{:.orange}
- Internal tech sharing: host or join tech-sharing sessions at your company.
- Community talks: regular community meetups are often looking for speakers.
- Talks at large conferences → can lead to offers for offline lectures / course production.

### Markdown Presentation Tools

Preparing a talk is a headache just thinking about making the slides. In that case, you can easily make slides with Markdown. Give it a try when you practice presenting in your study group!

- [Marp](https://marp.app/){:.none target="_blank"}: not many features, but simple and intuitive to use.
- [Slidev](https://sli.dev/){:.none target="_blank"}: supports animations, code highlighting, and more.

## Wrapping Up

Trying to do everything from the start can be overwhelming. It's better to start with one or two methods that suit you and expand gradually. For example, start by subscribing to tech blogs you're interested in and signing up for newsletters. Then you can grow your network by joining study groups or attending seminars, and move on to sharing your knowledge through blogging or giving talks.

Finally, **the foundation of all growth is consistency**{:.yellow}. I hope you'll grow step by step by learning a little every day, keeping your information up to date, and engaging with the community. I hope this post is a small help on your developer journey.

Thank you!😊
