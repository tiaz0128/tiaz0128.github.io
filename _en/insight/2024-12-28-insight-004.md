## 2024 in Review

2024 felt like being lost in pitch darkness. It was probably the worst economy I've seen since becoming an adult. Maybe it feels even worse because the developer boom was so recent?

One thing is certain: 2024 was a string of genuinely hard days. Even so, I'm writing this 2024 retrospective to look back on myself, still wandering around trying to find a way through it all.

- Crossing the Death Valley
- Seeing, hearing, feeling

## Crossing the Death Valley

Next April will mark three full years at my current company. A lot has happened in that time, but this time the thought suddenly struck me: "The company might actually disappear."

Many people left the company this year, and those of us who stayed had no choice but to keep moving forward, picking up the work of those who left. We were standing right in the middle of the Death Valley (VALLEY OF DEATH). Even within our group of affiliated companies, it was a period when our reason for existing was constantly being tested, and we had to prove it.

![Death Valley](/assets/img/content/insight/004/001.webp){:.img-m}

*> Source: [The Korea Economic Daily - Survival rate of Korean ventures in the "Valley of Death": 51%](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2023062808831){:target="_blank"}*{:.img-caption}

All this time, we'd been developing with less than a tenth of our competitors' headcount. And I thought about a lot of things. "Is it right to keep doing this? Are we even competitive?" The thought that came up most of all was:

> "Is what I'm building actually useful?"

Fortunately, this year our PoCs (Proof of Concept) started to bear fruit. We began to establish our place within the group. That was possible precisely because the people who stayed filled the gaps even more densely. I'm grateful to my teammates for all their hard work.

Of course, it's not all hopeful spring days yet. In 2025, the mood is likely to stay the same, or maybe even get worse. We may have just gotten over the first hump.

Well, it doesn't matter. I'll just keep quietly moving forward, today as always.

## Seeing, Hearing, Feeling

Personally, in 2024 I saw, heard, and felt change through countless channels. I invested my personal time and went all over the place. I took part in various hands-on courses and attended online webinars and offline seminars.

![2024 overview](/assets/img/content/insight/004/004.webp){:.img-m}

`> I sure got around a lot in 2024.`{:.img-caption}

It's painfully obvious, but the biggest trends of 2024 boil down to two things.

- Cloud
- AI

### Cloud

As we moved into the cloud era, the domain of "infrastructure" became easily accessible to anyone. I was reminded once again that, regardless of your field, the ability to work with the cloud directly is now a basic requirement.

### AI

Every seminar I went to this year was, well, all about AI. That's how much every company and every person was putting this field first. Progress in AI is truly astonishing. In a matter of months, AI technology was advancing several steps at a time.

Using AI in web services wasn't the whole story, either. I could feel that all tools are moving beyond their simple utility and evolving into partners that communicate and interact with humans = agents.
