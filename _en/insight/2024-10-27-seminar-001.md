Here's my honest take on volunteering at [PyCon 2024](https://2024.pycon.kr/){:target="_blank"}.

## A Day in the Life of Jabong

Jabong (Korean slang for a volunteer) set out at dawn, when the air was still cold. After all, he was about to take part in a big conference he'd never been to before — as a volunteer, no less. Everyone was meeting for the first time at the venue by 8:30 a.m. But what now? He'd shown up way too early.

It wasn't even 8 yet, but the venue was already bustling with people. Most of them were **people in blue T-shirts rushing around**. Jabong, a capital-`I` introvert, spent a whole year's worth of social energy to say hello to the people in blue T-shirts.

"Hi... I'm a volunteer."

## Why on Earth? Paying to Volunteer?

Sitting in a slightly chaotic office, Jabong ate Subway for breakfast. Out of nowhere, he remembered a conversation with a friend.

["Wait, you're paying 80,000 won just to volunteer? Why?"](https://blog.pycon.kr/2017/06/14/everybody-pays/){:target="_blank"}

Honestly, sitting there awkwardly and forcing down a breakfast that stuck in his throat, all Jabong could think was:

**"Ugh... why did I sign up for this?"**

But Jabong was curious. Before coming to PyCon, he'd been curious about the people he'd met at a Python seminar back in July.

## Python Korea User Group

At the [seminar held in Gangnam in July](https://event-us.kr/pythonkorea/event/87803){:.none target="_blank"}, the future Jabong — a capital-`I` introvert, of course — sat there awkwardly. Even during the networking break between sessions, he just stared at his phone. But during that short break, [someone I didn't really know](https://www.youtube.com/c/todaycode){:target="_blank"} kept going on and on~

"'Zen of Python' — you can only get it here! 25,000 won... supports the Python community..."

Well, I do love Python, so I bought one as if under a spell. Then, after the sessions, dreading the real networking time, I hurriedly packed up to sneak out. While I was waiting for the elevator, he came all the way out to bow and see me off. That's when I started to wonder.

**"Why do those people go that far?"**{:.orange}

Every one of those people was here, wearing a blue T-shirt. They were the ['PyCon Organizing Committee'](https://2024.pycon.kr/about/organizing-team){:target="_blank"} — "Pajunwi" for short in Korean. They spend a whole year preparing PyCon, then host and run the conference.

## So, How Was It?

"A bit rough? But hey, it's fine."

Honestly, some things were a little sloppy, and there were plenty of rough edges. I could fully understand the attendees' complaints. If I'd been an attendee, I might have been annoyed too. Still, I remember everyone saying, "It's okay. We understand." Thank you so much.

I sort of got why the people preparing PyCon — which isn't even their day job — work so hard to prepare and run it for an entire year. It was tough, but it was a far better experience than I expected, and I met great people. A special thank-you to the [other volunteers](https://2024.pycon.kr/about/volunteer){:target="_blank"} who toughed it out with me!

![The volunteers](/assets/img/content/insight/001/003.webp)

`> My fellow volunteers, who all worked so hard together!`{:.img-caption}

## Wrapping Up

> "PyCon is an event run entirely by people who receive no financial gain, purely to broaden the reach of the open-source programming language Python and to energize its community."

PyCon 2024 was great because it brought together such a wide variety of people. Because I took part as a volunteer, I felt firsthand [how different it is from other developer events](https://2024.pycon.kr/about/pyconkr2024){:target="_blank"}. It was a truly great experience. If you've read this far, I strongly recommend joining next year as a **PyCon 2025 volunteer**.

![PyCon 2024 volunteer](/assets/img/content/insight/001/002.webp){:.img-s}

`> I'm glad I volunteered.`{:.img-caption}

Finally, this PyCon 2024 volunteer will be back next year, at the very least as a PyCon 2025 volunteer! See you!

## Afterword

### 2025-02-08

In the end, the PyCon 2024 volunteer... applied to join the [2025 PyCon Organizing Committee](https://x.com/PyConKR/status/1873725443880489411){:.none target="_blank"}! 😊

I'm an ordinary developer. That's why I hope I can keep developing in Python for years to come. I realized I need to do what I can, for my own sake. I'm going to start various activities to help broaden Python's reach.

Thank you!
