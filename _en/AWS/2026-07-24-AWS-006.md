## One Picture Just Wasn't Enough to Explain Things

I've drawn a lot of architecture diagrams while studying the cloud. Preparing for AWS certifications, writing blog posts, explaining things to my team: the picture always came first.

But every time I drew one, something bothered me. When a single picture holds a VPC, subnets, a bunch of services and arrows all at once, **the person who drew it understands it, but the person looking at it doesn't know where to start.**

> "The request comes into the load balancer → goes through the Auto Scaling group → and on to the DB"

Getting that flow across with a single still picture turned out to be harder than I expected.

There are already plenty of good diagramming tools. They're great for "drawing", but what I really needed was **a tool for walking someone through what I'd drawn, step by step**. So I built one.

👉 [https://howtodraw.cloud](https://howtodraw.cloud)

## Not Just a Drawing Tool, but an 'Explaining' Tool

The feature I cared about most is **'Story'**.

Once the diagram is done, you create steps in the order you want to explain things. Then at each step, **only the relevant parts are lit up, the rest is dimmed, and a caption follows along.**

- **Step 1:** The user's request comes in here
- **Step 2:** It goes through authentication here
- **Step 3:** And then it heads to the DB

Even a complex architecture doesn't have to be shown all at once; you can **break it into pieces in the order that makes sense** and explain it that way. In short, here's how it's used:

- A story exports straight to a **captioned video (MP4)** that you can post right away to YouTube, Reels or Shorts (16:9 / 9:16 / 1:1, 720p·1080p, captions burned in or as `.srt`).
- As the story plays, you can **build the diagram up piece by piece**, or in a given step **gather** just the related objects onto a clean background.
- **Share it as a link or an embed**, and the recipient can click nodes and zoom in to explore it themselves.

## What Features Went In

I made the "drawing" part as fast and simple as I could, too. **No login, no install**: you can start right in the browser.

- **Multi-cloud palette** — AWS / GCP / Azure tabs, services grouped by category, real vendor icons (recolorable) + Lucide line icons. You can also drop your own SVG or PNG onto the canvas.
- **draw.io-style nesting** — Drop a service inside a boundary like a VPC or subnet and it becomes a child of it; drag it out and it's released.
- **Editable orthogonal connectors** — Grab a leg and drag it, and the bends you need appear and disappear on their own. Arrows, line styles, colors, thickness, labels, even animation.
- **Story · Export** — The story feature above, plus a **dependency-free single HTML file** (an interactive viewer where clicking opens details), high-resolution PNG, and MP4.
- **Save · Share** — A diagram is just a **JSON document**. You can pack the whole diagram into a single link (URL fragment) and share it **with no server and no account**, and if you log in you can save to the cloud and share in the gallery.

## What I Want to Do Next

I'm still polishing things, but there are two directions I'm clear on.

### Quizzes with pictures

If Story is a feature for **explaining**, I thought diagrams could also serve as a learning tool: turning a question's text into a diagram and explaining the answer visually.

I hope people studying for certifications use it to lock in concepts visually, and to make and share questions with each other.

### Open source

I designed the editor (`fe/`) **with this in mind** from the start. It works on its own without a backend, and **I plan to release the editor as a separate open-source repo**.

## What I'm Aiming For

No grand plans. Three lines will do.

- **A small community.** The goal is for anyone to draw their architecture, share it, and **learn together**.
- **Monetization isn't on the table.** No ads, no paid plans.
- And honestly, **even if nobody else uses it, I'll keep using it myself lol**

When you're organizing concepts while studying for a certification, making blog posts or video content, or onboarding your team onto an architecture — I hope it helps anyone who wants to **go from drawing to explaining in one go**.
